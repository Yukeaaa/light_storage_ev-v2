Abstract 
With the rapid development and intelligent transformation of power systems, the 
scale and complexity of power equipment have significantly increased. Traditional fault 
analysis methods, such as those relying on expert experience and rule-based systems, 
struggle to address the challenges posed by the growing diversity of equipment fault 
types and the demands of processing vast amounts of data. Although large language 
model have demonstrated remarkable success across various domains, their direct 
application to power equipment fault analysis remains challenging. One key issue is the 
"hallucination" problem, where these models may generate erroneous fault inferences 
when 
data 
is 
insufficient. 
Furthermore, 
the 
complex 
and 
dynamic 
structural 
characteristics of power equipment expose a "visual blind spot" in traditional large 
language models, limiting their effectiveness in reasoning and analysis. To address these 
issues, this paper proposes a novel approach that integrates knowledge graphs with large 
language models to provide a more intelligent and efficient solution for power 
equipment fault analysis. The study encompasses the following contributions: 
This paper presents propose a semantic-encoding based approach for the design of 
subgraph matching algorithms. The method constructs a knowledge map of power 
equipment faults based on fault data, designs a semantic coding based on the 
BioBERT-Hybrid dual-channel modei and a structural coding approach based on the 
synergy of structural feature quantisation and multi-scale structural aggregation, and 
then the semantic and graph-structural coding are fused for vector representation. The 
vectors are then matched for relevant subgraphs using a FAISS-Hybrid similarity 
computation approach under both speed and accuracy considerations. The subgraph 
information is used as supplementary knowledge to enhance the performance of the 
large language model in fault analysis. 
This paper propose a methodology for the design of an agent based fault analysis 
report generation methodology. In the study of refining outlines in the pre-writing phase, 
a method for synthesising topic outlines based on retrieval and multi-perspective 
questioning is proposed. The method enables a simulated dialogue between different 
viewpoints, asking questions from multiple perspectives and collating their information 
to create an outline. In the generation of content for fault analysis reports, a framework 
for writing analyses based on expansion-reflection is proposed. The framework mimics 
the iterative human process of expansion and reflection to deepen the understanding and 
knowledge of failure analysis. Failure analysis report generation is jointly completed 
based on the outline as well as the specific content. 
This paper designs and implements a knowledge graph and large models based 
fault analysis platform system for power equipment. The platform is developed using a 
front-end and back-end separation architecture, with the front-end using technology
